# Patent research: Photoshop adjustment kinds (#250)

Engineering research record for the Photoshop adjustment kinds added under #250 ([adjustment-kinds.md](adjustment-kinds.md)). The binding summary is the "Adjustment layers" section of [legal-constraints.md](legal-constraints.md); read that first. Each entry carries its date and depth: a keyword sweep cannot prove a negative. This is an engineering record of the designs and of the published patents considered.

- **Black & White reviewed for fixed user weights (2026-10-20, keyword sweep plus claim reads)**: Photoshop's Black & White (CS3, 2007) mixes six user weights for the reds, yellows, greens, cyans, blues and magentas; Patchy's calibrated form is the smallest channel plus the weighted middle-minus-smallest and largest-minus-middle spans (docs/adjustment-kinds.md). User-weighted channel mixing for grayscale conversion is long-standing prior art (Channel Mixer monochrome, prepress conversion), and no Adobe claim on fixed hue-sector weights was found (Google Patents and Justia, Adobe assignee, "grayscale", "black and white", "color to grayscale", 2006 onward). Live patents read: Adobe US 7706606 B1 ("Fast, adaptive color to grayscale conversion", priority 2006-05-31, to 2029-02-03) claims determining color clusters from the image, gray values for color-space points from weighted target differences to those clusters, and mapping pixels through them; that is content-derived conversion, which Patchy never does (no Auto button, no gray derived from image statistics). Apple US 9092893 B2 ("Method and interface for converting images to grayscale", priority 2012-03-06, to 2032-09-27) derives the red, green and blue weights from one control value along a parameterized path; Patchy keeps six independent sliders and never drives them from one control. Apple's abandoned US 2015/0109323 (interactive black and white editing) and its live grain patent US 9652867 (film grain fields, to 2035) are absent; adding film grain to the adjustment would need its own check.
- **Color Lookup adjustment layers reviewed for stored tables (2026-10-20, short claim-level check)**: Photoshop's Color Lookup (CS6, 2012) applies a 3D LUT (`.cube`, `.3dl`, `.look`) or an ICC abstract or device-link profile stored in the layer's `clrL` block. Applying a sampled 3D table by trilinear or tetrahedral interpolation is decades-old color-management practice (prepress scanner LUTs, ICC since 1994, Little CMS), and no in-force patent was found on applying a stored LUT as an adjustment layer: Adobe's adjustment-layer patents US 5974198 and US 6185342 expired in 2016, and the sweep (Adobe assignee, "3D LUT", "color lookup table", "three-dimensional lookup table", "adjustment layer", 2012 onward) found no Adobe claim on it. Live patents read, none describing the stored-table design: Adobe US 7859722 B1 (to 2029-07-03) claims rewriting a conversion LUT's gray-axis entries to pure gray and smoothing the near-gray entries, so Patchy applies the stored table unmodified (never edits table nodes) and builds ICC transforms for embedded profiles with Little CMS's white and gray fix-ups disabled; Adobe US 9857953 B2 (to 2036-04-08) claims transferring a style image's color and tone to an input image through a covariance-limited chrominance transfer plus a tone curve fitted by non-linear optimization, and Patchy never derives a look from another image. Not Adobe and absent: HP US 8743141 (CLUT node-adjustment smoothing, fee-lapsed 2026) and Intel US 2017/0061926 (non-uniform LUT sampling, abandoned). Re-check before adding look generation from image content, look matching between images, LUT editing, or LUT export.
- **Vibrance (2026-10-20, keyword sweep; built as a fixed curve)**: no Adobe patent on Photoshop's Vibrance (2007/2008) was found. The claims of Apple's "vibrancy" family (US 9105121, US 9159144, US 9299168, priority 2012-03-06, to 2033) require choosing which editing controls to present from the content type detected at an image location the user selects; Patchy's Vibrance presents the same two sliders for every image and detects no content. Pixar US 8396290 requires color correction curves chosen from an estimate of scene illumination, which Patchy does not make. Patchy's design ([adjustment-kinds.md](adjustment-kinds.md), "Vibrance"): each pixel's gain and pivot gray are fixed functions of that pixel's own largest channel, saturation and hue, with a fixed weaker boost for red-to-orange hues; no skin-tone, face, region or other content detection, and no other pixel or image statistic is read. A change that adds skin-tone or other content detection needs a claim-level read first.
- **Selective Color (2026-10-20, keyword sweep; built after confirmation)**: no in-force patent on Photoshop's Selective Color method was found. The closest relative, Agfa US 6058207 (selective correction around local gamuts, priority 1995), expired in 2016; prepress drum-scanner selective correction (1970s and 1980s) is general prior art. A possible patent issue had been raised; on 2026-10-20 it was confirmed that no known patent blocks Selective Color, and implementing it with a Photoshop calibration run, like the other kinds, was approved. Patchy's implementation is fitted to Photoshop's own output under #250; FFmpeg's LGPL `selectivecolor` filter, the one public reverse-engineered model, was not used or copied.
