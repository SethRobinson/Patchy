# Patent research: Photoshop adjustment kinds (#250)

Engineering research record for the Photoshop adjustment kinds added under #250 ([adjustment-kinds.md](adjustment-kinds.md)). The binding summary is the "Adjustment layers" section of [legal-constraints.md](legal-constraints.md); read that first. Each entry carries its date and depth: a keyword sweep cannot prove a negative. This is an engineering record of the designs and of the published patents considered.

- **Black & White reviewed for fixed user weights (2026-10-20, keyword sweep plus claim reads)**: Photoshop's Black & White (CS3, 2007) mixes six user weights for the reds, yellows, greens, cyans, blues and magentas; Patchy's calibrated form is the smallest channel plus the weighted middle-minus-smallest and largest-minus-middle spans (docs/adjustment-kinds.md). User-weighted channel mixing for grayscale conversion is long-standing prior art (Channel Mixer monochrome, prepress conversion), and no Adobe claim on fixed hue-sector weights was found (Google Patents and Justia, Adobe assignee, "grayscale", "black and white", "color to grayscale", 2006 onward). Live patents read: Adobe US 7706606 B1 ("Fast, adaptive color to grayscale conversion", priority 2006-05-31, to 2029-02-03) claims determining color clusters from the image, gray values for color-space points from weighted target differences to those clusters, and mapping pixels through them; that is content-derived conversion, which Patchy never does (no Auto button, no gray derived from image statistics). Apple US 9092893 B2 ("Method and interface for converting images to grayscale", priority 2012-03-06, to 2032-09-27) derives the red, green and blue weights from one control value along a parameterized path; Patchy keeps six independent sliders and never drives them from one control. Apple's abandoned US 2015/0109323 (interactive black and white editing) and its live grain patent US 9652867 (film grain fields, to 2035) are absent; adding film grain to the adjustment would need its own check.
- **Color Lookup adjustment layers reviewed for stored tables (2026-10-20, short claim-level check)**: Photoshop's Color Lookup (CS6, 2012) applies a 3D LUT (`.cube`, `.3dl`, `.look`) or an ICC abstract or device-link profile stored in the layer's `clrL` block. Applying a sampled 3D table by trilinear or tetrahedral interpolation is decades-old color-management practice (prepress scanner LUTs, ICC since 1994, Little CMS), and no in-force patent was found on applying a stored LUT as an adjustment layer: Adobe's adjustment-layer patents US 5974198 and US 6185342 expired in 2016, and the sweep (Adobe assignee, "3D LUT", "color lookup table", "three-dimensional lookup table", "adjustment layer", 2012 onward) found no Adobe claim on it. Live patents read, none describing the stored-table design: Adobe US 7859722 B1 (to 2029-07-03) claims rewriting a conversion LUT's gray-axis entries to pure gray and smoothing the near-gray entries, so Patchy applies the stored table unmodified (never edits table nodes) and builds ICC transforms for embedded profiles with Little CMS's white and gray fix-ups disabled; Adobe US 9857953 B2 (to 2036-04-08) claims transferring a style image's color and tone to an input image through a covariance-limited chrominance transfer plus a tone curve fitted by non-linear optimization, and Patchy never derives a look from another image. Not Adobe and absent: HP US 8743141 (CLUT node-adjustment smoothing, fee-lapsed 2026) and Intel US 2017/0061926 (non-uniform LUT sampling, abandoned). Re-check before adding look generation from image content, look matching between images, LUT editing, or LUT export.
- **Vibrance (2026-10-20, keyword sweep; built as a fixed curve)**: no Adobe patent on Photoshop's Vibrance (2007/2008) was found. The claims of Apple's "vibrancy" family (US 9105121, US 9159144, US 9299168, priority 2012-03-06, to 2033) require choosing which editing controls to present from the content type detected at an image location the user selects; Patchy's Vibrance presents the same two sliders for every image and detects no content. Pixar US 8396290 requires color correction curves chosen from an estimate of scene illumination, which Patchy does not make. Patchy's design ([adjustment-kinds.md](adjustment-kinds.md), "Vibrance"): each pixel's gain and pivot gray are fixed functions of that pixel's own largest channel, saturation and hue, with a fixed weaker boost for red-to-orange hues; no skin-tone, face, region or other content detection, and no other pixel or image statistic is read. A change that adds skin-tone or other content detection needs a claim-level read first. Apple US 8638338, found later, is compared in the next entry.
- **Apple US 8638338 and US 9639965 compared with Vibrance (2026-11-02, claim-level comparison)**: found in the GEGL filter review ([gegl-filters.md](gegl-filters.md#legal-review)), not in the October sweep. US 8638338 B2 (Ben Weiss, Apple, filed 2008-02-11, granted 2014-01-28; 4th, 8th and 12th-year fees paid, the last on 2025-07-16; in force to 2032-03-22 with PTA) and its continuation US 9639965 B2 (filed 2014-01-27; fees paid through year 8; expires 2028-09-29); claim text from Google Patents, with the USPTO fee events. The specification's vibrancy slider gives pixels outside a skin-tone region of the color space "the specified amount of saturation" directly and pixels inside it a smaller amount, scaled by their deviation from exact skin-tone colors.
  - US 8638338 claims 1, 8, 12, 16 and 21 (and dependents 2 to 24) need one slider that "(i) for each pixel having a set of component color values that falls within a sub-region of the color space, non-uniformly adjusts the pixel's set of component color values based on the single adjustment ... and a deviation of the pixel's set of component color values from a set of component color values of a particular color sample in the sub-region" (8 and 16: skin-tone samples; 12: the sample at the center; 16 and 21: the nearest of several), and "(ii) for each pixel ... that falls outside of the sub-region ..., uniformly adjusts the pixel's set of component color values based on the single adjustment". Not met: Patchy keeps no reference color, and (ii) fails on its own, since outside any hue range the boost gain still varies with each pixel's saturation and brightness, and the reduction gain with its saturation.
  - US 9639965 claims 1, 8 and 14 drop the uniformity but need "determining a nearest matching color sample of a plurality of color samples in the sub-region" (14: "selects one or more particular color samples") and a degree of adjustment set by the deviation from it. Not met: no color samples and no nearest-sample search. Patchy's one hue term, the fixed 17 % weaker boost over red-to-orange hues, is a function of the pixel's own hue position at every saturation and brightness, not a distance from a stored color.
  - US 8638338 claim 25 (the broad one) needs "a region selector for selecting at least a portion of the displayed image; and a single adjustable UI item that, in response to a single adjustment to the single adjustable UI item: (i) uniformly adjust a set of component color values of each of a first set of pixels within the selected portion of the image based on the single adjustment of the single adjustable UI item, and (ii) the non-uniformly adjust a set of component color values of a second set of pixels within the selected portion based on the single adjustment of the single adjustable UI item." Patchy's selection is a region selector and the Vibrance slider one UI item (Saturation is a second one); the question is "uniformly". The specification equates it with the plain saturation control: the need is an effect "applied uniformly (i.e., linearly)" to some regions, prior applications "uniformly increase saturation across an entire image or portions of an image", and pixels failing the criteria get the specified amount directly (process 1400). Read with the specification, the first set gets the slider's amount unmodified and the second a per-pixel modified amount. Patchy gives no pixel the unmodified amount: each boosted pixel's gain is scaled by its own saturation, brightness and hue (zero at gray and at full saturation), each reduced pixel's by `1 - 0.75 dark s^1.788`, and only the gray limit gets the full amount, where there is no chroma to change. One case is recorded here: in a reduction every fully saturated color (smallest channel 0) brighter than linear 0.0488 (about sRGB 62) gets the same gain `1 - 0.25 |v|` about its own largest channel. That case is one gain for identical colors, which every per-pixel color map gives. Published earlier work describing a related technique, more than a year before the filing: Photoshop's Hue/Saturation with an edited color range (Photoshop CS2 user guide, 2005) gives colors inside the range the slider's full amount and the falloff a tapering one, inside a selection, from one slider.
  - Patchy's Hue/Saturation band sliders (a full-weight plateau with feather ramps, [adjustments-calibration.md](adjustments-calibration.md#per-hue-range-bands)) give colors inside a band the slider's full amount and colors in the ramps a tapering one, the form of Photoshop's Hue/Saturation color ranges before 2007 (published earlier work describing the same technique: Photoshop CS2 user guide, 2005). Open item (2026-11-04): add a dated copy of that guide (an archived Photoshop CS2 or earlier user guide) to this record. Keep the bands to Photoshop's range-and-falloff form.
  - GEGL's `gegl:vibrance` (0.4.72; probed as a black box through `patchy-gegl-host --run` on 553 CIELAB colors at Vibrance -5 to +5, its source unread) adds about the same chroma, close to the Vibrance value in CIELAB units, to every pixel, keeping hue and lightness (within 0.2 degree and 0.06 L) with no hue-dependent range; grays stay gray, and a reduction stops at gray as a plain saturation boost stops at the gamut edge. Every pixel gets the same unmodulated change, with no second set adjusted differently; its Saturation property is a plain scale. It may be offered.
  - Boundary ([legal-constraints.md](legal-constraints.md#adjustment-layers)) until 2032-03-22: Vibrance's gain stays a fixed function of each pixel's own values. Never a slider that gives colors outside a protected color region the plain amount and colors inside it an amount scaled by their distance from reference colors (skin tones or any other), and never a nearest-reference-color search, without a new review.
- **Selective Color (2026-10-20, keyword sweep; built after confirmation)**: no in-force patent on Photoshop's Selective Color method was found. The closest relative, Agfa US 6058207 (selective correction around local gamuts, priority 1995), expired in 2016; prepress drum-scanner selective correction (1970s and 1980s) is general prior art. A possible patent issue had been raised; on 2026-10-20 it was confirmed that no known patent blocks Selective Color, and implementing it with a Photoshop calibration run, like the other kinds, was approved. Patchy's implementation is fitted to Photoshop's own output under #250; FFmpeg's LGPL `selectivecolor` filter, the one public reverse-engineered model, was not used or copied.
