# Text recognition: blocks

Split from [ocr.md](ocr.md): how recognized lines become blocks, what one multi-line text layer is made from. The binding entry is "Text recognition" in [legal-constraints-scanning.md](legal-constraints-scanning.md#text-recognition).

## Joining lines

`ocr_layout.{hpp,cpp}`: `group_lines_into_blocks` turns lines into blocks, what becomes one multi-line text layer; `lines_as_blocks` is the one-layer-per-line choice. Two lines of the same orientation join when they are the same size, their centres are at most 2.2 times their mean thickness apart across the lines (and at least half the thinner one), and along the lines they overlap by at least half the shorter one. Lines rotated more than 2 degrees stay alone. "The same size" is their lengths per character within 30% of each other (and their thicknesses within a factor of 2.2), or, for a line of fewer than four characters, their thicknesses within 30%. Both measures exist because an engine's line box hugs the ink: a line with no descenders is a fifth thinner than its neighbour at the same size (a thickness-only rule split a paragraph there, at the threshold, under the Mac's text rendering), and the empty gap between two lines of 36 px text set 50 px apart is 17 px after a line with descenders and 24 px after one without (a gap rule split the same paragraph on Windows), while length per character and the 50 px pitch do not move. A `Block` carries its lines in reading order (top to bottom; vertical text right to left), its box, `alignment` (the edge its lines share most closely: `Start`, `Center` or `End` along the reading direction), `line_size` (median thickness) and `line_pitch` (median centre-to-centre distance). Blocks come back horizontal ones first, top to bottom then left to right, then vertical ones right to left.

## One line per row

**A block holds one line per row.** A block is drawn as one text layer with one leading, so it is a stack of rows. Two lines share a row when their boxes overlap across the lines by at least half the thinner one. When two lines of one row would both join the same line (a title and a label beside it over a paragraph, the buttons under one, two columns under a line that spans them), two boxes cannot say which of them continues that line's block, so neither join is made; row neighbours that still meet through a longer chain each stand alone and the rest of that set is joined again without them. An engine that reports a whole row as one line (Windows) never meets this; the PP-OCR detector reports each piece of a row, and before this rule a screenshot of a list in an interface came back as one block of 26 lines with a median pitch of 1 px, drawn as one pile (`ocr_blocks_hold_one_line_per_row` holds those boxes, `ui_recognize_text_keeps_row_neighbours_apart` the layers). What stays the grouping's limit: a lone label close above or below a paragraph still joins it and takes the paragraph's leading and edge, so it can sit some pixels off. "One layer per line" places every line on its own box.

## Measurements

What sets the join constants, measured November 2026 on two sets of PP-OCR line boxes (`ocr_blocks_keep_labels_off_paragraphs` holds the first) and the clean benchmark pages ([ocr-benchmark.md](ocr-benchmark.md)):

- **The boxes must hug the ink.** With the padded rectangle the recognizer reads (1.5 times the ink's height on a lone line), the first entry's title in a dark interface screenshot joined the smaller line above it (centres 42.8 px apart, under 2.2 x 27) and was drawn about 200 px off, and the buttons under two paragraphs joined them. The engine now reports its lines about where the ink is ([ocr-engines.md](ocr-engines.md#pp-ocr), "Line boxes"), which is also what the Windows engine and the drawn test pages report, the boxes these constants were first set on.
- **Mean thickness, not the thicker one's.** A short word on a button gets a box thicker than its size (22 px for "Fix it" under 13 to 17 px paragraph lines, 47 px below the paragraph's last line): against the thicker line it joined the paragraph (47 < 2.2 x 22), against the mean it does not (2.2 x 17.8 = 39). Lines of one paragraph have about the same thickness, so the change moves nothing for them, and the thinner line still reaches a thicker neighbour (a line of x-height letters between two full ones: 46 px apart at 18 and 34 px, against 2.2 x 26 = 57).
- **Where 2.2 sits.** Largest centre distance over mean thickness between consecutive lines of one paragraph: 1.6 to 1.8 for English, Latin and Russian at 33 and 44 px, 1.9 Korean, 2.1 Japanese (Mincho 44 px, Gothic 33 px: the detector's regions are thinnest in dense square text). Smallest between lines of the screenshot that are the same size, overlap and must stay apart: 2.3 (a title or a row of labels over a paragraph). With 0.2 instead of 0.25 as the engine's `line_grow` the Japanese pages split into 5 and 15 blocks; at 0.3 a label of the screenshot joins a paragraph again. The margin on each side is small; a page set tighter or looser than these can join or split where a reader would not.
- **Length per character stays at 30%.** Consecutive lines of one paragraph on the benchmark pages differ by up to 17% (Japanese lines that end in punctuation), so the title's 19% difference from the line above cannot be told by it.

## Inputs

**The only inputs are the boxes and character counts of lines, tested two at a time** (do they join, do they share a row). No line is classified as starting or continuing a paragraph, and nothing about the page (type, margins, indentation, layout) enters. Two paragraphs with no gap between them are one block: that is this grouping's stated limit, not a bug to fix with indentation tests.
