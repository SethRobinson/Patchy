# Patent research: Color Replacement tool (GitHub 720)

Comparison of 2026-11-13 behind the "Color Replacement tool" entry in [legal-constraints-selection.md](legal-constraints-selection.md#color-replacement-tool); design: [color-replacement.md](color-replacement.md). Both claim sets were read in full from the USPTO Patent Public Search service on 2026-11-13 ([patent-research.md](patent-research.md#reading-patent-text)). End dates are the filing date plus 20 years plus the term adjustment that service reports, before any terminal disclaimer; fee status was not checked.

- **US 11223744 B2** (Adobe, "Local color range selection", filed 2017-10-17, no continuity data, term adjustment 46 days: 2037-12-02; first read in part for [Color Range](patent-research-selection.md#color-range-github-709)).
  - Claim 1 (method): obtaining an image whose pixels hold values of parameters defining color and tone; receiving user input of a user drawing around a coarsely-defined region that encompasses an adjustment region; generating a spatial mask with a value per pixel saying whether it is in that region; generating a range mask with a value per pixel saying whether its value of a user-specified parameter is within a range; receiving a request to adjust the value of a parameter; and, in response, an adjustment of that parameter weighted by a combination of the spatial mask value and the range mask value.
  - Claim 7 (medium): claim 1's elements, and the range mask generated by receiving a Gaussian width value and generating a 3D smoothing function centered at a point of a color space. Claim 12 (apparatus): a color range mask with an indication of a point model or an area model in Lab, a range mask file, an image received from photographic equipment, and a normalization (each channel's range expanded to the full range, conversion to Lab, the Lab ranges expanded again); its adjustment is based on the range mask, and the spatial mask appears only in dependent claim 13.
  - Dependent claims: a color range mask (2); the Lab point or area model, a 3D smoothing function and a Gaussian width (3 to 5); spatial and range mask files (6, 11); a brush, a linear gradient or a radial gradient as the local adjustment tool (9); the product of the two masks (10); holding a mouse button and moving the mouse (14); a slider that sets the range mask (15); luminance histogram and color range normalization (16, 17); a tube-shaped region of a 3D color space (18).
  - Patchy's tool (`src/core/color_replace.cpp`): a stroke is a row of brush dabs. Each dab takes one sample (the stroke-start pixel under its center, the one under the first dab, or the background swatch), kept as one point in display RGB. A pixel matches when the sum of its squared channel differences from that point is within a fixed limit (the Magic Wand's metric); with Anti-alias a straight line carries the match to zero a quarter of that limit further. The matching pixels, or only those joined to the dab's center, are repainted with the foreground color through the Hue, Saturation, Color or Luminosity blend math, weighted by the tip's coverage, the match and the selection. The pixels are written as the dab lands. The live stroke keeps the stroke-start pixels and each pixel's largest weight so far; both are dropped at release, and nothing about the stroke is stored in the document.
  - Against claim 1: the dab footprints are a user drawing over a region and the tip coverage is a per-pixel value for it; the tolerance test is a per-pixel value saying whether the pixel's color is within a range around the sample; the weight is the product of the two. The tool has no separate request to adjust a parameter after the masks exist: the foreground color and the Mode are set before the stroke, each dab writes at once, and nothing can be adjusted afterwards. Apart from that order, each element of claim 1 has a counterpart, and no design limit separates the tool from the claim.
  - Against claims 7 and 12: the match holds no Gaussian or other smoothing function, no Lab model, no normalization and no mask file.
  - Earlier publications of the tool's form (listed for the decision from the products' histories, not checked against copies): E1, Photoshop 5.5 (1999), Background Eraser: a brush that erases the pixels under the tip that match a sampled color, with Sampling (Continuous, Once, Background Swatch), Limits (Discontiguous, Contiguous, Find Edges) and Tolerance. E2, Photoshop CS (2003), Color Replacement tool: the same options with a Mode of Hue, Saturation, Color or Luminosity and Anti-alias, repainting with the foreground color. E3, Paint Shop Pro 5 (1998), Color Replacer: a brush that replaces the background color with the foreground color within a tolerance. All three precede the 2017-10-17 filing date by 14 years or more.
- **US 8218860 B1** (Adobe, "Method and system for replacing color ranges in an image", filed 2008-08-28, no continuity data, term adjustment 986 days: 2031-05-11; found by a title search for Adobe's color range titles). Independent claims 1, 12 and 23 (method, system, medium): creating a histogram of the image's hue values; determining a prominent hue range from that histogram; determining a replacement hue range; and changing each pixel of the prominent range to the corresponding hue of the replacement range. Dependent claims add a black and white rendering of everything else, a downsampled image for the histogram, a conversion to HSV, Gaussian smoothing of the histogram and a peak walk for each range.
  - Patchy's tool forms no histogram and no other statistic of the image, finds no prominent color, and maps no range onto another: the color to replace is the pixel under the pointer or the background swatch, and every replaced pixel takes the one foreground color through a blend mode. The elements present: a color replaced by another within a tolerance.
  - Boundary: the color to replace is never chosen from the image's content (a hue histogram, a prominent or dominant color, a peak search), and one range is never remapped onto another range.
- Other records: Color Range's boundary under US 11223744 stays as written for the command ([record](patent-research-selection.md#color-range-github-709)). The local adjustment brushes keep their limit ([patent-research.md](patent-research.md)): Dodge, Burn, Sponge, Blur and Sharpen never measure a color or infer a boundary. The self-adaptive brush application US 2007/0188510 named there was abandoned and has no claims in force; this tool's Once sampling does read the color at the stroke's start.
- Background Eraser (GitHub 727, 2026-11-15; design: [eraser-tools.md](eraser-tools.md)): Patchy's Background Eraser is the tool E1 describes, built on the same code as the tool above (`src/core/color_match_stroke.cpp`, `src/core/background_eraser.cpp`). Each dab takes one sample the same way, applies the same match and the same Limits, and lowers the alpha of the matching stroke-start pixels by the weight as the dab lands (under a transparency lock the pixels move toward the background color instead, the Eraser's existing rule). Against claim 1 of US 11223744 the comparison is the one above with "erase" in place of "repaint": the same counterparts, and the same absence of a separate request to adjust a parameter after the masks exist. Against claims 7 and 12, and against US 8218860: no Gaussian, Lab model, normalization, mask file, histogram or prominent color. Options the 1999 form is not recorded here as having stay out: no correction of the color of the remaining pixels, no second color test (a protected foreground color). No new search was run; the tool rests on the decision below. The Magic Eraser added with it erases the Fill tool's flood region (one clicked color, the Magic Wand's tolerance, an optional contiguity test) and holds no brush, sample model or stored range.
- Protect Foreground Color (GitHub 727 follow-up, searched and decided 2026-11-17; design: [eraser-tools.md](eraser-tools.md#protect-foreground-color)). The 2026-11-15 note above listed a protected foreground color as an option the 1999 form "is not recorded here as having". The search below corrects that: the option is part of that form.
  - Publication: Photoshop 5.5's Background Eraser Options palette shows a Protect Foreground Color check box beside Tolerance and Sampling. Found in the Internet Archive's scanned books through its full-text search on 2026-11-17 (the search returns the matching lines; the books are lending copies and were not read in full): "Master Photoshop 5.5 Visually" (IDG Books, 1999), "Photoshop 5.5 for Windows and Macintosh" (Peachpit Press, 1999: "to protect a particular color from erasure, check the Protect Foreground Color box"), "Fundamental Photoshop 5.5" and "The Photoshop 5/5.5 Wow! Book" (2000), and the Photoshop 6 user guide ("Select Protect Foreground Color to prevent the erasure of areas that match the foreground color"). Alt+click as the way to pick that color is in the same books.
  - **Adobe's "Smart erasure brush" family** (found by title search; all four read in full from the USPTO Patent Public Search service on 2026-11-17): US 6434269 B1 (filed 1999-04-26, no term adjustment: 2019-04-26), US 6791573 B2 (division, filed 2002-06-04: 2019-04-26), US 7400763 B2 (continuation, filed 2004-07-30, term adjustment 748 days: 2021-05-13) and US 7627168 B2 (continuation, filed 2008-06-18: 2019-04-26). All four end dates are past. This is the tool E1 itself: tip regions along a stroke, an erasure color sampled at the center of the tip or at the start of the stroke or fixed, a color match function with a tolerance, pixels made transparent by their match, each tip application recorded in the image before the next. The 1999 specification also describes the protected color: "a color to not erase, a 'keep' color, can be selected", and "both an erasure color and a keep color can be defined. The erasure color is used in the decontamination process, while the keep color can be used in evaluating which pixels to erase." Claims 17 and 38 of US 6791573 recite a keep color established at each tip application, a match of each pixel with it, and decontaminating the colors of the pixels made partly transparent. Every independent claim of the four patents (15 in all) recites removing a contribution of the erasure color from the pixels that stay, or decontaminating them, which Patchy's tool does not do. The specification, published 2002-08-13, is one more dated publication of a second, kept color test.
  - **Corel's "Adaptive region editing tool" family** (found by a full-text search for "background eraser"; independent claims read from the same service on 2026-11-17): US 7826668 B1 (filed 2004-02-17, term adjustment 1175 days: 2027-05-07, the only member whose computed date is not past), US 7782338 B1 (filed 2004-09-30, 584 days: 2026-05-07) with its continuations US 8081196 and US 8115782 (2024-09-30), and US 7609894 B2 (continuation-in-part of the first, 705 days: 2026-01-22) with US 7940988 and US 8233725 (2024-02-17). Claim 1 of US 7826668: sampling pixels in a first predefined region of a tool impression to determine a first distribution of a pixel property; sampling pixels in a second predefined region of the tool impression to determine a second distribution; and editing at least one pixel within the tool impression based on the first and second distributions. The other members add an assisted tolerance computed from the distributions, a sampling region that grows, and a uniformity test. None of the seven mentions a protected or kept color.
  - Patchy's tool against claim 1 of US 7826668: the erase sample is the one stroke-start pixel under the dab's center (or the pixel under the first dab, or the background swatch); no region of the tip is sampled into a distribution, and there is no second sampled region. With Protect Foreground Color the second color is the foreground swatch, a color the user picked beforehand, read from no region of the tool impression. The Tolerance is the user's number and is never computed from the pixels under the tip.
  - Boundary: the protected color stays a color the user names (the foreground swatch, a script's argument). It is never sampled from a region of the tip (a ring, the rim, an area the user marks inside the tip), never an average, range or other statistic of pixels, and the erase sample stays one pixel, never a measured region of the tip. The Tolerance is never adjusted from the image. No color correction of the pixels that stay.
  - Other searches the same day (USPTO full text, granted patents filed since 2006-11-17 and applications since 2020): "protect foreground color" (no patent; one unrelated application), "keep color" with erasure and brush (the Adobe family and two unrelated), an eraser brush near a protected, kept, preserved or excluded color with a tolerance or color match (eight: the Adobe family's last member, Behr's room painting application family US 9857888 and four continuations, whose claims recite a paint bucket, a paint brush, an eraser that removes color that bled and masking tools on a room image, and Geomagical's scene depth patents; none recites a color-matching eraser or a kept color). Adobe titles with "eras": the family above, US 9916017 (erasing vector strokes) and an unrelated channel coding patent.
  - Not verified: the books against full copies (search lines only); fee status of US 7826668 (the service gives none); foreign members; CPC class sweeps (the service's class queries returned nothing in the forms tried, [patent-research.md](patent-research.md#reading-patent-text)).
  - Decision (2026-11-17): Protect Foreground Color is built as part of the 1999 form the tool already rests on: a check box, the foreground swatch as the kept color, and one fixed rule per pixel: a pixel at least as near (plain RGB distance) to the kept color as to the dab's sample is not erased, with a straight-line ramp a quarter of the tolerance distance wide. The rule estimates no alpha and corrects no color. Re-check US 7826668 at 2027-05-07; the boundary above binds until a new review.
  - Adobe US 8050498 (the Quick Select entry, to 2029-11-03), re-read the same day because the option sorts pixels into kept and erased during a stroke: each of its independent claims recites piecewise constant regional cost values (a foreground, a background and a bias cost) and classifying pixels into coherent regions that include pixels outside the stroke's sample. The tool has no cost values and no regions, and writes only pixels under the tip by a per-pixel distance test.
- Not verified: E1 to E3 against copies of the manuals; fee status of either patent; continuations beyond the title searches by assignee of 2026-11-13 (titles with "color range", "range mask", "color replacement", "replacing color"); class queries for brush tools with a color tolerance by other assignees (none run).
- Decision (2026-11-13): the tool is built and ships as built, in main and in releases. For claim 1 of US 11223744 no design limit separates it; it rests on the earlier publications E1 to E3, the same kind of decision as the Crop ones of 2026-11-11 ([patent-research-crop.md](patent-research-crop.md)). The tool stays in that published form: one sampled point in display RGB with the Magic Wand's tolerance metric, no Lab model, no Gaussian, no histogram or range normalization, pixels painted at once, no mask or range stored, nothing adjustable afterwards, and no color chosen from the image's content. A change to any of those needs a new review first. Re-check 2031-05-11 and 2037-12-02.
