# Patent research: Color Replacement tool (GitHub 720)

Comparison of 2026-11-13 behind the "Color Replacement tool" entry in [legal-constraints-selection.md](legal-constraints-selection.md#color-replacement-tool); design: [color-replacement.md](color-replacement.md). Both claim sets were read in full from the USPTO Patent Public Search service on 2026-11-13 ([patent-research.md](patent-research.md#reading-patent-text)). End dates are the filing date plus 20 years plus the term adjustment that service reports, before any terminal disclaimer; fee status was not checked.

- **US 11223744 B2** (Adobe, "Local color range selection", filed 2017-10-17, no continuity data, term adjustment 46 days: 2037-12-02; first read in part for [Color Range](patent-research-selection.md#color-range-github-709)).
  - Claim 1 (method): obtaining an image whose pixels hold values of parameters defining color and tone; receiving user input of a user drawing around a coarsely-defined region that encompasses an adjustment region; generating a spatial mask with a value per pixel saying whether it is in that region; generating a range mask with a value per pixel saying whether its value of a user-specified parameter is within a range; receiving a request to adjust the value of a parameter; and, in response, an adjustment of that parameter weighted by a combination of the spatial mask value and the range mask value.
  - Claim 7 (medium): claim 1's elements, and the range mask generated by receiving a Gaussian width value and generating a 3D smoothing function centered at a point of a color space. Claim 12 (apparatus): a color range mask with an indication of a point model or an area model in Lab, a range mask file, an image received from photographic equipment, and a normalization (each channel's range expanded to the full range, conversion to Lab, the Lab ranges expanded again); its adjustment is based on the range mask, and the spatial mask appears only in dependent claim 13.
  - Dependent claims: a color range mask (2); the Lab point or area model, a 3D smoothing function and a Gaussian width (3 to 5); spatial and range mask files (6, 11); a brush, a linear gradient or a radial gradient as the local adjustment tool (9); the product of the two masks (10); holding a mouse button and moving the mouse (14); a slider that sets the range mask (15); luminance histogram and color range normalization (16, 17); a tube-shaped region of a 3D color space (18).
  - Patchy's tool (`src/core/color_replace.cpp`): a stroke is a row of brush dabs. Each dab takes one sample (the stroke-start pixel under its center, the one under the first dab, or the background swatch), kept as one point in display RGB. A pixel matches when the sum of its squared channel differences from that point is within a fixed limit (the Magic Wand's metric); with Anti-alias a straight line carries the match to zero a quarter of that limit further. The matching pixels, or only those joined to the dab's center, are repainted with the foreground color through the Hue, Saturation, Color or Luminosity blend math, weighted by the tip's coverage, the match and the selection. The pixels are written as the dab lands. The live stroke keeps the stroke-start pixels and each pixel's largest weight so far; both are dropped at release, and nothing about the stroke is stored in the document.
  - Against claim 1: the dab footprints are a user drawing over a region and the tip coverage is a per-pixel value for it; the tolerance test is a per-pixel value saying whether the pixel's color is within a range around the sample; the weight is the product of the two. The tool has no separate request to adjust a parameter after the masks exist: the foreground color and the Mode are set before the stroke, each dab writes at once, and nothing can be adjusted afterwards. Apart from that order, each element of claim 1 has a counterpart, and no design limit separates the tool from the claim.
  - Against claims 7 and 12: the match holds no Gaussian or other smoothing function, no Lab model, no normalization and no mask file.
  - Earlier publications of the tool's form (listed for the decision from the products' histories, not checked against copies): E1, Photoshop 5.5 (1999), Background Eraser: a brush that erases the pixels under the tip that match a sampled color, with Sampling (Continuous, Once, Background Swatch), Limits (Discontiguous, Contiguous, Find Edges) and Tolerance. E2, Photoshop CS (2003), Color Replacement tool: the same options with a Mode of Hue, Saturation, Color or Luminosity and Anti-alias, repainting with the foreground color. E3, Paint Shop Pro 5 (1998), Color Replacer: a brush that replaces the background color with the foreground color within a tolerance. All three precede the 2017-10-17 filing date by 14 years or more.
- **US 8218860 B1** (Adobe, "Method and system for replacing color ranges in an image", filed 2008-08-28, no continuity data, term adjustment 986 days: 2031-05-11; found by a title search for Adobe's color range titles). Independent claims 1, 12 and 23 (method, system, medium): creating a histogram of the image's hue values; determining a prominent hue range from that histogram; determining a replacement hue range; and changing each pixel of the prominent range to the corresponding hue of the replacement range. Dependent claims add a black and white rendering of everything else, a downsampled image for the histogram, a conversion to HSV, Gaussian smoothing of the histogram and a peak walk for each range.
  - Patchy's tool forms no histogram and no other statistic of the image, finds no prominent color, and maps no range onto another: the color to replace is the pixel under the pointer or the background swatch, and every replaced pixel takes the one foreground color through a blend mode. The elements present: a color replaced by another within a tolerance.
  - Boundary: the color to replace is never chosen from the image's content (a hue histogram, a prominent or dominant color, a peak search), and one range is never remapped onto another range.
- Other records: Color Range's boundary under US 11223744 stays as written for the command ([record](patent-research-selection.md#color-range-github-709)). The local adjustment brushes keep their limit ([patent-research.md](patent-research.md)): Dodge, Burn, Sponge, Blur and Sharpen never measure a color or infer a boundary. The self-adaptive brush application US 2007/0188510 named there was abandoned and has no claims in force; this tool's Once sampling does read the color at the stroke's start.
- Not verified: E1 to E3 against copies of the manuals; fee status of either patent; continuations beyond the title searches by assignee of 2026-11-13 (titles with "color range", "range mask", "color replacement", "replacing color"); class queries for brush tools with a color tolerance by other assignees (none run).
- Decision (2026-11-13): the tool is built and ships as built, in main and in releases. For claim 1 of US 11223744 no design limit separates it; it rests on the earlier publications E1 to E3, the same kind of decision as the Crop ones of 2026-11-11 ([patent-research-crop.md](patent-research-crop.md)). The tool stays in that published form: one sampled point in display RGB with the Magic Wand's tolerance metric, no Lab model, no Gaussian, no histogram or range normalization, pixels painted at once, no mask or range stored, nothing adjustable afterwards, and no color chosen from the image's content. A change to any of those needs a new review first. Re-check 2031-05-11 and 2037-12-02.
