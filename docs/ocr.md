# Text recognition (OCR)

Reading text out of pixels. This doc owns the engine library (`src/ocr`, target `patchy_ocr`) and the rules every entry point follows. Status: the library, the Windows engine, recognized text layers, the script calls and the Type menu commands are in; the Import PDF option, the downloadable model engine and searchable PDF output are being added on top, each with its own section here as it lands.

## Rules that bind every entry point

The binding entry is "Text recognition" in [legal-constraints-scanning.md](legal-constraints-scanning.md#text-recognition) (claim comparisons: [patent-research-text-recognition.md](patent-research-text-recognition.md)). The rules every caller meets:

- **Recognition runs only on an explicit user action** (a command, a ticked option, a script call). Nothing recognizes in the background, and nothing downloads unasked.
- **The engine and the language are the user's picks**, read from settings (`ocr/engine`, `ocr/language`), never derived from the image. No code tries several recognizers and keeps the best.
- **No typeface identification.** Nothing names or matches a font from pixels, and recognition never uses a detected font.
- **Results never leave their feature.** Nothing from recognition feeds Divide Scanned Photos, book page detection, cutting, straightening or tone choices.
- **Engines never run on the GUI thread** (below).

## Library (`src/ocr`, Qt-free)

`ocr/ocr_engine.hpp` is the whole public surface:

- `ImageView` (pixels, size, stride, `PixelLayout` of Gray8, Rgb8, Rgba8 or Bgra8; straight alpha, read over white).
- `Result` = `lines` plus an optional page text angle. A `Line` has UTF-8 `text`, an axis-aligned `box`, a `quad` (its corners in the text's own frame: top left, top right, bottom right, bottom left), `angle_degrees` (clockwise, y down), `vertical` (characters run top to bottom) and `words` (text, box, quad). Everything is in the pixels of the image handed in. `Result::text()` joins lines with `\n`.
- `Engine`: `id()` (a permanent token persisted in settings and used by scripts: `windows`, `ppocr`, `fake`), `display_name()` (English fallback; the UI translates the ids it knows), `languages()` (asked fresh on every call: a `Language` is a tag, BCP-47 for an operating system engine, plus a display name), `default_language()` (from the user's system or app language, never from an image), `unavailable()` (`None`, `NoLanguages`, `NotDownloaded`, `Failed`) with `unavailable_reason()` (an English diagnostic), and `open(language_tag)`.
- `Recognizer::recognize(image, cancel)`: one engine opened for one language, held for a batch of images. One caller thread at a time, **never the GUI thread**: an engine may block for seconds, and the Windows engine waits on an asynchronous operation, which a single-threaded apartment does not allow. It throws `RecognitionCancelled` when its `Cancel` was requested and `std::runtime_error` (an English diagnostic) when the engine fails. An image with no text is an empty result, never an error.
- `engines()` lists every engine this build and machine has, available or not: the operating system's (`detail::platform_engines`, `ocr_engine_stub.cpp` where there is none), then whatever `add_engine_provider` registered (how an engine outside this library joins). `find_engine(id)` looks one up. Both are thread-safe.

### Oversized images

`ocr_bands.{hpp,cpp}`, shared by every engine with a size limit M (`recognize_in_bands`, `plan_bands`). The rule is fixed geometry:

1. An image wider than M is scaled so its width is M (height in proportion, rounded to nearest).
2. If the scaled height still exceeds M it is read as full-width bands of M rows that overlap by V = max(16, M / 8) rows (at most M / 2); every band but the last starts at a multiple of M - V and the last ends at the bottom.
3. A line read in two neighbouring bands is kept once: by the band on whose side of the overlap's midpoint the line's vertical centre falls.

Lines come back in the original image's pixels, in band order then engine order. No text is compared and no pixel is looked at to decide anything. Against a limit of 2600 (older Windows), a Letter page at 300 ppi is two unscaled bands and at 600 ppi it is scaled to 2600 wide first; against 10000 both are one unscaled tile. `ocr_image.{hpp,cpp}` makes each band straight from its source rows (`scaled_rows_bgra`: opaque BGRA over white, each scaled pixel the rounded mean of whole source pixels, integer math only), so a page is never copied whole at the scaled size.

### Blocks

`ocr_layout.{hpp,cpp}`: `group_lines_into_blocks` turns lines into blocks, what becomes one multi-line text layer; `lines_as_blocks` is the one-layer-per-line choice. Two lines of the same orientation join when they are the same size, their centres are at most 2.2 thicknesses of the thicker one apart across the lines (and at least half the thinner one), and along the lines they overlap by at least half the shorter one. Lines rotated more than 2 degrees stay alone. "The same size" is their lengths per character within 30% of each other (and their thicknesses within a factor of 2.2), or, for a line of fewer than four characters, their thicknesses within 30%. Both measures exist because an engine's line box hugs the ink: a line with no descenders is a fifth thinner than its neighbour at the same size (a thickness-only rule split a paragraph there, at the threshold, under the Mac's text rendering), and the empty gap between two lines of 36 px text set 50 px apart is 17 px after a line with descenders and 24 px after one without (a gap rule split the same paragraph on Windows), while length per character and the 50 px pitch do not move. A `Block` carries its lines in reading order (top to bottom; vertical text right to left), its box, `alignment` (the edge its lines share most closely: `Start`, `Center` or `End` along the reading direction), `line_size` (median thickness) and `line_pitch` (median centre-to-centre distance). Blocks come back horizontal ones first, top to bottom then left to right, then vertical ones right to left.

**The only inputs are the boxes and character counts of two lines at a time.** No line is classified as starting or continuing a paragraph, and nothing about the page (type, margins, indentation, layout) enters. Two paragraphs with no gap between them are one block: that is this grouping's stated limit, not a bug to fix with indentation tests.

## Running a recognition (UI side)

`ui/ocr_recognition.{hpp,cpp}` and `MainWindow::run_text_recognition` (main_window_ocr.cpp) are the one path every entry point takes:

- **Source pixels** (`ocr_source_image`): one layer rendered on its own (`automatic_selection_layer_image`: any kind, with its mask and effects, cropped to where it has pixels) or the document's composite, optionally cropped to a rectangle; always 8-bit RGBA whatever the document's depth. `OcrSourceImage::origin` is where the image's top left sits in the document.
- **Which engine** (`resolve_ocr_choice`): the engine and language the caller names, else the stored picks `ocr/engine` and `ocr/language` while they still exist, else the first available engine and its default language. A named engine or language that does not exist is an error, never a silent fallback.
- **Off the GUI thread** (`recognize_on_worker`): the engine runs through `launch_async` while the calling thread waits in 15 ms slices, calling the caller's `pump` each time (a dialog turns its event loop and watches Cancel; a script feeds its watchdog and watches for an interrupt). The wait turns the event loop, so afterwards the session is looked up again by id and a closed or resized document is refused.
- **What it makes** (`OcrLayerMode`, tokens persisted and used by scripts: `none`, `visible`, `replace`, `hidden`): nothing, text layers over the image, text layers with the original hidden, or text layers in a hidden group. `begin_change` runs once right before the document changes: the caller records its one undo step there (`push_undo_snapshot`, or a script's `prepare_mutation`).

## Text layers

`ui/ocr_text_layers.{hpp,cpp}` (`build_recognized_text_layers`) turns a result into drawn, placed text; `MainWindow::make_recognized_text_group` (main_window.cpp, beside the text metadata writers it needs) wraps them in layers; `insert_recognized_text_group` places the group.

- **The group** is a Pass Through group named "Recognized Text", inserted above the layer that was read (the top of the stack for the whole image), with the first block in reading order on top. It carries `patchy.ocr.group` = `"<engine id> <language tag>"` (`layer_is_recognized_text_group`), so a later PDF export finds recognized text whatever the group is renamed to; each text layer carries `patchy.ocr.line` until it is rasterized. Both are session metadata, never written to PSD as layer data.
- **A layer per block** (`OcrGrouping::Blocks`, token `blocks`, the default) or per line (`lines`). A block (see "Blocks") is one point-text layer whose lines are separated by line breaks, one paragraph each, with the block's alignment and, when it has more than one line, a fixed leading equal to the measured line pitch. A fixed leading only lays out under the Photoshop line model, so such a layer carries `patchy.text.layout = photoshop`, exactly as a layer whose leading was set in the Character panel does. A vertical block is vertical type, its columns in reading order.
- **The text metadata is an editor commit's**: one style run and the paragraph runs go through `psd::serialize_patchy_text_runs` and `serialize_patchy_paragraph_runs`, and the layer is stored with `store_patchy_text_metadata` and `store_text_layout_metrics`, so the Type tool edits it like any typed layer and its name follows its text.
- **Font**: the caller's family, which is the user's pick (the Type tool's font unless a dialog or script names one). Nothing identifies a typeface.
- **Size** (with matching on): the story is laid out at a probe size with the real text pipeline (`build_text_render_plan`), each line's natural length is compared with the recognized line's length, and the block takes the median of those fits, rounded to a whole pixel. The fit is kept only within 0.5 to 2.2 times what the line thickness suggests (thickness / 0.9); outside that, and for lines of fewer than three characters, the thickness decides. A length fit is then checked against what it really draws: the block's ink is measured along the reading direction against its recognized lines, and when it is more than 2% off the block is drawn once more at the size the difference calls for (one correction, no loop). Line lengths are read from the laid-out `QTextDocument` (each block's `QTextLayout` lines), never from `TextRenderPlan::line_render_items`: the plan lists line items only for the layouts that place lines itself (box text, the Photoshop line model), so plain point text has none, and reading them made every single-line block fall back to its thickness without a word (64 px text came out at 67 or 68, 6% wide; `ui_recognize_text_fits_lines_to_boxes` now holds the ink within 3.5% of the drawn line).
- **Color** (`sampled_text_color`): the background is the per-channel median of the one-pixel ring outside the line's box, the text the mean of the fifth of the box's pixels farthest from it; black when nothing in the box stands 24 levels a channel from the background. The longest line speaks for its block.
- **Italic** (`measured_slant_degrees`): the box's column profile is sheared in two-degree steps from -4 to 24 and the sharpest wins, upright unless a lean is measurably sharper; 7 degrees or more picks the family's italic face. Horizontal, upright lines only.
- **With matching off**: the size and color the caller gives (the Type tool's), upright.
- **Placement**: the block is drawn once at its final size and moved by whole pixels so the centre of its ink sits on the centre of its lines' boxes. Upright text is plain point text at those bounds. A line the engine reported turned (more than 2 degrees) is drawn through that rotation, cropped to its ink, and keeps the rotation plus the shift as `patchy.text.transform`, as imported PDF text does.
- **Bit depth**: rasters are drawn 8-bit and converted to the document's depth.
- **"Show text and hide original"** (`replace`): the layer that was read is hidden (for the whole image, every other top-level layer), and the group gets an opaque white "Paper" layer at its bottom covering the area read. Nothing is erased, filled or healed: showing the original again restores it exactly.

## Scripting

`script_ocr.cpp` (`patchy.ocr`, `layer.recognizeText`, `doc.recognizeText`; types and guide in `scripts/bundled`). The calls are strict (an unknown option, engine or language throws before anything is read), run unattended (so the MCP connector's `execute_script` can read text out of an image), never download anything and never write settings. What a call leaves unsaid comes from the Type tool: font, and with `matchStyle: false` its size and color. Layers arrive under the run's undo step (`prepare_mutation`); an interrupt during the wait cancels the engine and changes nothing.

## Type menu commands

`MainWindow::recognize_text`, `copy_text_from_image` and `run_recognize_text_choice` (main_window_ocr.cpp); the rows sit after Paste Lorem Ipsum. Both need only a document, finish an open text edit first, and are on the web build's desktop-only list.

- **Type > Recognize Text (OCR)...** (`typeRecognizeTextAction`, `type.recognize_text`) opens the dialog below and runs what it returns.
- **Type > Copy Text from Image** (`typeCopyTextFromImageAction`, `type.copy_text_from_image`) asks nothing: it reads the selection, else the active layer, else the image, with the stored engine and language, puts the text on the clipboard (lines joined by `\n`) and reports "Copied N lines". It adds no layer and no undo step, whatever the dialog's stored Result is. Only when no engine can run does it open the dialog, which says why.
- **Sources** (`OcrSource`, tokens persisted in `ocr/source`: `layer`, `selection`, `image`): the active layer on its own; everything visible inside the selection's bounding rectangle (the selection's shape is not used); everything visible. A selection or image read puts the group on top of the stack; a layer read puts it above that layer.
- **The wait.** The engine runs through `run_text_recognition` with a window-modal `recognizeTextProgressDialog` (Cancel only, no percentage: engines report none) that the pump shows after 400 ms, so the built-in engine usually finishes without one. Until the dialog is up, and after Cancel hides it, the pump turns the event loop with `ExcludeUserInputEvents`: input waits in the queue, so nothing edits the document under the engine. Never pump with plain `processEvents()` there.
- **One undo step**, "Recognize Text", taken only when layers are about to be added. Cancel, an engine failure, no text found and the clipboard result change nothing and say so on the status bar.

## Dialogs

- **`OcrEnginePicker`** (`ui/ocr_controls.{hpp,cpp}`) is the Engine and Language pair every dialog shares, with object names `<prefix>EngineCombo`, `<prefix>LanguageCombo`, `<prefix>UnavailableNote` and `<prefix>LanguageSettingsButton`. It starts on the stored picks while they exist and can run, else the first engine that can. `store()` writes `ocr/engine` and `ocr/language`; the owning dialog calls it when accepted, and nothing else writes those keys. It asks the engines again when the application becomes active, so a language added in Windows Settings shows up without reopening the dialog.
- **Nothing can run**: the note says which case it is. No engine in this build (macOS, Linux): text recognition uses the engine built into Windows 10 and 11. The Windows engine with no recognizer language: where to add one, with **Open Language Settings** (`ms-settings:regionlanguage`; `set_ocr_language_settings_opener_for_testing` replaces the launch in tests). The dialog still opens in both cases, with its OK button off.
- **Recognize Text** (`recognizeTextDialog`, `request_recognize_text` in `ui/recognize_text_dialog.{hpp,cpp}`): the picker; Read (`recognizeTextSourceCombo`, rows only for sources that exist; a selection is the starting row while there is one, whatever was stored); Result (`recognizeTextResultCombo`: "Text layers over the image" `visible`, "Show text and hide original" `replace`, "Hidden text layers" `hidden`, "Copy the text to the clipboard" `clipboard`); Text layers (`recognizeTextGroupingCombo`: `blocks`, `lines`); Font (`recognizeTextFontCombo`); "Match size, color and slant from the image" (`recognizeTextMatchStyleCheck`). The last three are off for the clipboard result. Accepting stores `ocr/result`, `ocr/source`, `ocr/grouping`, `ocr/matchStyle` and `ocr/fontFamily`; a Font row left on the Type tool's font stores nothing, so it follows the tool next time.
- **Preferences > Tools > Text Recognition** (`preferencesOcrGroup`): the same picker with the prefix `preferencesOcr`, stored when Preferences is accepted. Not in the web build.

## Windows engine

`ocr_engine_win.cpp`, engine id `windows`: Windows.Media.Ocr (Windows 10 and 11) through C++/WinRT. It is the only translation unit that includes WinRT headers (they come with the Windows SDK; `runtimeobject` is linked; no `windows.h`, no Qt), and no WinRT type leaves it: `winrt::hresult_error` is rethrown as `std::runtime_error` with the system's message.

- **Languages** are the recognizer languages Windows has installed (`OcrEngine::AvailableRecognizerLanguages`, tag and Windows' own display name), asked fresh each time. None installed is `Unavailable::NoLanguages`: the user adds one in Settings > Time & language > Language & region. `default_language()` is the first of the user's Windows profile languages that has a recognizer.
- **Threads.** Every WinRT call runs on a thread the file owns, in the multithreaded apartment. A `Recognizer` keeps one (`ApartmentThread`) for its life: it creates the engine there, runs each `recognize` there while the caller waits, and releases the engine there before the thread leaves its apartment. The listing calls use a short-lived thread each. Pool threads are never used for WinRT: some already sit in a single-threaded apartment (the WIC decoders), where `init_apartment` throws.
- **The apartment is kept alive once used** (`keep_apartment_alive`: one parked, detached thread that never leaves it). C++/WinRT caches each activation factory in static storage and `uninit_apartment` does not clear that cache, so when the last thread left the apartment COM unloaded the recognizer's library under the cached factory and the next call crashed in unloaded code (the second listing call, before this existed). Never remove the parked thread or call `clear_factory_cache` instead: a factory in use is skipped by the clear and then never cleared at all.
- **Size limit.** `OcrEngine::MaxImageDimension`, read at run time, is the M of "Oversized images": 10000 on the Windows 11 build this was developed on (November 2026), 2600 on older Windows. A tile smaller than 64 pixels on a side is padded with white first.
- **Cancel.** The recognize call waits on one `Completed` handler in 20 ms slices; a requested `Cancel` cancels the operation and throws `RecognitionCancelled`. Never poll with `wait_for` in a loop: each call assigns the operation's single Completed delegate again, and the second assignment fails with "A delegate was assigned when not allowed" (any image that takes longer than one slice).
- **Boxes.** A line has no rectangle of its own in this API: its box is the union of its words'. When the engine reports a text angle, its word rectangles are in the frame of the text turned upright about the image centre, so each corner is turned back by that angle (clockwise, y down) to land on the pixels; `Line::quad` and `Word::quad` are those corners, `box` their bound, and every line carries the angle. `ui_ocr_windows_engine_reports_rotated_text_if_available` pins the direction and the frame against text drawn at a known angle.
- **Japanese and Chinese.** The engine's "words" are single characters there and its line text puts a space between every two of them, so for a `ja` or `zh` recognizer a line's text is its words joined without spaces.
- **Vertical lines.** A line whose words run down the page, in a box more than twice as tall as wide, is marked `vertical`: read from the engine's own boxes. The Japanese recognizer reads vertical columns as columns, right to left (`ui_ocr_windows_engine_reads_japanese_if_available`: two drawn columns came back as two exact lines). It decides between horizontal and vertical reading from the page, though, and a lone short horizontal line gives it too little: one 11-character line on an otherwise empty image came back as three "columns" of character fragments, while the same line in a three-line paragraph read exactly.

## Testing

- **Real engine**: `tests/ui/ocr_ui_tests.cpp` (prefix `ui_ocr_`) draws text and reads it back through the Windows engine: upright English with word and line boxes on the drawn pixels, a blank and a tiny image, text turned 8 degrees, a page taller than the size limit whose numbered rows must each come back once, in order, and a Japanese paragraph and two vertical Japanese columns. They skip without the engine, the recognizer language or a font to draw with.
- **Layers and scripts**: `tests/ui/ocr_layers_tests.cpp` (`ui_recognize_text_`, `ui_script_ocr_`, `ui_script_recognize_text_`) draws text, hands the fake engine the drawn boxes, and checks the layers against the drawn pixels: size and placement, one size and kept line breaks per block, centred blocks, sampled colors, the italic face, vertical and turned lines, the hidden and replace modes, one undo step, strict options, and an engine that is slow or fails.
- **Commands and dialogs**: `tests/ui/ocr_command_tests.cpp` drives the two Type menu commands through their actions: the dialog with no engine and with an engine that has no language, the options it stores and starts on again, one group in one undo step, the clipboard result, a selection read, Cancel in the progress dialog (a 20 second fake delay that must end early), no text and an engine failure, a 16-bit document, Copy Text from Image without a dialog, and the Preferences picker. `tests/ui/ocr_page_fixture.hpp` holds the drawn page (`OcrTestPage`) these and the layer tests share.
- **New OCR test files** are declared in `tests/ui/ocr_test_support.hpp` and gathered by `ocr_ui_tests()`, so adding one does not touch the suite-wide group header (which recompiles every UI test file).
- **The UI suite starts with no engine**: `tests/ui/main.cpp` installs an empty list, so nothing depends on the machine's languages; a test installs a fake under `ScopedOcrEngines`, or the real list under `ScopedRealOcrEngines` (`tests/ui/ocr_test_support.hpp`).
- **In-process**: `make_fake_engine(FakeEngineConfig)` (`ocr_fake_engine.hpp`) builds a scripted engine: lines with boxes and text, languages (none makes it `NoLanguages`), a size limit (it goes through the real banding), a per-band delay for cancel tests, a page angle, an unavailable state, a failure message. It records each call (language, image size, the tiles it was handed). `set_engines_for_testing(factory)` replaces the whole list; an empty list is "no engine on this system", an empty function restores the real one. Tests install it under a guard that restores on exit.
- **A real build without an engine** (`--run-script`, visual QA): `PATCHY_FAKE_OCR="x,y,w,h=text;v:x,y,w,h=text"` makes the list the fake alone (boxes in image pixels, `v:` for a vertical line, `\uXXXX` escapes; an empty value is an engine that finds nothing), with `PATCHY_FAKE_OCR_LANGUAGES` (`tag=Name;...` or `none`), `PATCHY_FAKE_OCR_MAX_DIMENSION`, `PATCHY_FAKE_OCR_DELAY_MS` and `PATCHY_FAKE_OCR_ANGLE`. The spec is inline, so no file entry point exists.
- Core tests (`tests/core/ocr_tests.cpp`, prefix `ocr_`): the registry and its hooks, the pinned band plan, lines in an overlap reported once, a wide image scaled and mapped back, the area average, alpha over white, cancel before and during a read, the environment spec, and block grouping (spacing, alignment, vertical order).
