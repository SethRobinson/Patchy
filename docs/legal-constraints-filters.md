# Binding filter, retouching and G'MIC constraints

Split from [legal-constraints.md](legal-constraints.md): the binding entries for filters and Smart Objects (filter renderers, the auto adjustments, content-aware fill and exemplar inpainting, the healing tools, Content-Aware Move, Content-Aware Scale, Red Eye) and the vendored G'MIC engine; the parent indexes each entry and links every claim-level record.

## Filters and Smart Objects

- **Surface Blur may not compute through a value histogram.** Median and Dust & Scratches keep exactly one window histogram updated one pixel value at a time; never introduce column, merged or slid histograms before the expirations (US 8594445, US 7920741). The calibrated outputs and constraint comments in `smart_filter_renderer.cpp` are binding.
- **Tilt-Shift Blur draws no boundary marks.** Its overlay is the center crosshair, the rotation line and the four handle dots (decided 2026-10-20; US 8971623, audit item 3). Never add a line or bar along a band edge before 2033, and keep every mark off the image perimeter. Pinned by `ui_filter_gallery_tilt_shift_overlay_has_no_boundary_marks`; the algorithm itself is clear.
- **Lens Blur and Iris Blur remain fixed and geometric.** Lens Blur is one explicitly selected aperture convolution with no depth inference, classification, highlight detection, or per-pixel kernel. Iris Blur computes one round blur and blends it through one explicit ellipse. Do not add multiple pins, combined blur patterns, Photoshop-style iris boundary widgets, variable-radius kernels, bokeh thresholds, learned depth/focal mattes, splatting or native `blurbTransform` authoring without a new review (US 8831371, US 9019310, US 8824793, US 12561768). Imported Blur Gallery stays preview-locked; Lens/Iris stay raster-only.
- **Liquify remains manual, generic, and raster-only.** Never add face detection, landmarks, automatic face regions, face-feature controls or deformation derived from a detected face without a new review (US 9646195). Do not invent or partially author Photoshop's native Liquify Smart Filter descriptor. Ordinary layers save the raster result, Smart Objects require rasterization, imported unsupported stacks stay byte-preserved.
- **Plastic Wrap keeps its fixed local height-field formula:** integer alpha-weighted luminance, box smoothing, gradient, and relief. Do not add material or lighting inference, classification, patch search or synthesis, or content-driven algorithm selection.
- **Lens Flare and Flame are Patchy's own designs** ([record](patent-research-fire.md)): no Photoshop lens or Flame presets or dialog layout; Flame makes no keyframes.
- **Unsharp Mask and Motion Blur remain fixed and user-directed.** Unsharp Mask uses one radius-selected low-pass plus explicit Amount and Threshold. Motion Blur uses one straight-line kernel from explicit Angle and Distance. Do not add ranking, inferred blur, automatic thresholds, patch search, deconvolution, shake estimation, tracking, optical flow, content-adaptive kernels, per-pixel paths or Blur Gallery-style widgets without a new review. Smart Sharpen may render only legacy Gaussian entries with Noise Reduction 0, as this Unsharp Mask; Sharpen Edges must keep its fixed threshold 64 and weight span 191 ([record](patent-research-smart-sharpen.md)).
- **The auto adjustments (Auto Tone, Auto Contrast, Auto Color) use only whole-image tonal statistics with fixed constants** (August 2026): one 256-bin histogram per channel (merged for Auto Contrast) plus per-channel means, a fixed 0.1% clip per end, and the fixed 128 neutral midtone target, applied through the shared levels transfer. No spatial or sliding-window histograms, no content classification, no automatic selection between algorithms, and no learned or content-adaptive parameters; any of those needs a new review. Kernel: `src/filters/auto_levels_math.{hpp,cpp}`, cited by its constraint comment. Prior art: [record](patent-research-filters.md).
- **Content-aware fill and exemplar inpainting** (claim-level review 2026-09-22, [record](patent-research-inpainting.md)). Classic exemplar-based inpainting is ALLOWED: fill the hole from its boundary inward in a fixed, deterministic priority order and, for each target patch, choose the best source patch by an EXHAUSTIVE scan of a bounded search window in the same image at the working resolution (integer distance metric, fixed tie-breaks, or Efros-Leung's own epsilon selection among the near-best candidates of that same scan) (expired Criminisi and Efros-Leung prior art). Content inspection for source choice is permitted in that form (`core/exemplar_inpaint.cpp`). Forbidden while the cited patents are active: (1) any nearest-neighbor-field search whose candidates come from neighbouring patches' current offsets (propagation) or from perturbing an offset (random search), iterated (Adobe US 8285055, to 2031-04-09; every independent claim needs both phases), so each target patch's search is a plain scan independent of other patches' chosen offsets; (2) precomputed pruned candidate lists, "patch loops", or belief-propagation reconstruction over patch labels (US 8340463, to 2031-10-24); (3) reshuffling, retargeting, or patch optimization under user feature or orientation constraints with model fitting and projection (US 8355592, to 2031-08-12); (4) computing a patch coordinate map at a downsampled resolution and upsampling it to fill at full resolution (US 9396530, to 2034-10-15): no coarse-to-fine offset maps; (5) machine-learned object boundaries or per-object masks (US 10825148, to 2039) and sources from other images (US 9697595 / US 10467739). Stay in that plain form: no patch rotation, scale, or mirror adaptation, no gradient-domain color adaptation, and no user-painted sampling region without a new check (Photoshop's 2018+ Content-Aware Fill workspace has unreviewed Adobe filings). Its distance metric may carry a fixed geometric term (a per-pixel penalty on the source offset), and its tone match is the recorded healing membrane applied to the low-pass band (frequency separation): Dirichlet interpolation of boundary values, never composited gradients. Its only randomness is the variation pick (`ExemplarInpaintOptions::attempt` > 0, the Remove Object dialog's Reroll; claim check 2026-09-24 in the record): a splitmix64 draw among the candidates within a fixed slack of the best distance, recomputed per target from a fresh exhaustive scan of the same windows and dropped after the copy, never stored, seeded from another patch, perturbed, or propagated. No candidate list may outlive its target, seed a neighbour, or narrow a later search. Also: no ML/GAN dust-and-scratch removal while the US 11393077 family is active; re-read US 12530821 before integrating generative output into Smart Filter stacks; never create a one-click merge-visible plus effects plus masked-copy operation without re-reading US 8655112.
- **Spot Healing, Remove Object, the Patch tool and the Healing Brush stay inside the classic-healing carve-out** (August to October 2026, [record](patent-research.md)). All blend with the healing membrane that expired US 6587592 taught (`core/heal_membrane.cpp`): destination-minus-source offsets from the region BOUNDARY interpolated harmonically across the interior by fixed deterministic relaxation, then added to the source texture. Never composite source gradients or build a Poisson guidance field (engineering boundary; audit record). Sources stay user-directed or shape-derived: Spot Healing uses one coherent rigid mapping chosen from the stroke footprint's shape alone (`core/spot_heal.cpp`, fixed candidate order, geometry-only validity scores, pixel content never read); Remove Object is the same mapping chosen from the SELECTION's shape plus the user's repeat count (`attempt`) indexing the geometrically valid candidates in their fixed order (never chosen or scored by pixel content); the Patch tool's only source is the user's drag offset, the drag showing only the moving outline (Source) or a raw ghost at the drop point (Destination), the region built geometrically once on release; the Healing Brush's is the user's Alt-click offset, healed once on release. Content-driven source selection may join them only as the exhaustive exemplar search the bullet above allows, and no live per-move classification or healing preview may be added while US 8050498 (live classify-and-display, to Nov 3, 2029) is active. In the Remove Object dialog a fill runs only on an explicit Reroll click, a slider release, or a settled slider/spin value (one coalesced run per discrete change), never per slider or pointer move, and the canvas shows the finished fill of the last run, not a live classification of input. Any on-canvas gizmo beyond the drag outline with its unprocessed content ghost and the plain context menu needs its own check.
- **No tool may show or write the content of the selection's current location in its original spot during a drag** (2026-11-03, [record](patent-research-content-aware-move.md), [design](content-aware-move.md)). Adobe US 9575641 and its continuation US 10332291 (to 2033) need that step in every independent claim; US 10332291 covers any fill method after it. So Content-Aware Move and the Patch tool show only the dashed outline, plus a raw ghost of the region at the pointer offset (never inside the original spot), and heal once on release. Because no independent claim of either patent is met without that drag step, the fill on release is free of them (2026-11-03 follow-up); Content-Aware Move fills the spot it leaves, in order, with (1) one exhaustive exemplar search for the whole hole (`exemplar_block_match`: the hole plus a band is one target patch, every translation in a bounded window scored by integer L1 in a fixed order, first smallest wins) healed in from that offset with the membrane, (2) the exemplar fill of the bullet above, (3) the shape-derived map; every search excludes the drop region and the hole. Its membranes fade to an edge band by a plain distance weight (`fade_heal_offsets_to_edge_band`). Still banned there: propagated or perturbed offsets, coarse-to-fine offset maps, gradient compositing, any heal or preview during the drag. Its Object Selection option (2026-11-03) is Select Object's one box solve on the release of a drawn outline, grown and feathered as plain selection edits before any drag; the fill and heal see only the resulting selection, never per-object masks or learned boundaries (US 10825148 needs a trained boundary-completion model and fills per object mask). Structure or Color adaptation settings and Transform on Drop each need a new check.
- **Content-Aware Scale (seam carving) is not built; no seam-based resize ships before 2027-03-06** (GitHub issue 681, research of 2026-11-09, [record](patent-research-content-aware-scale.md)). MERL US 7477800 (any minimal one-pixel seam from a gradient-magnitude energy image, and a seam-order map of an enlarged image with any energy) runs to 2027-03-06. From then a build works on a layer's whole pixel extent with per-pixel gradient or forward energy, one dynamic-programming seam search per seam, removal and duplicate-and-average insertion, an Amount mixed with plain scaling, and a protect mask taken only from the user's selection or a saved channel. Never, while the patent named runs: resize a selection or any region smaller than the layer with the rest unchanged (Adobe US 8160398, to 2031-02-16); weight energies by pixel values or colors, skin tones included (US 8290300, to 2031-05-12); generate a protect mask from energy and offer it for editing (US 8659622, to 2032-05-08); scale or resample along a seam (US 8218900, to 2031-05-09); solve the seam search in concurrent tiles (US 8581937, to 2031-06-17); move a selected object by carving ahead of it and growing behind it (US 8358876, to 2031-04-23); a per-edge or per-corner priority input on the resize box (US 8963960, to 2032-02-01); search seams on a downscaled energy map (Corel US 9111364, US 8483513, to 2032); and the other assignees' energy and seam-order variants the record lists (Xerox, Google, Michigan State, to about 2033) or local seam expansion sized to an inserted object (Adobe US 12437411). Face, object or saliency detection steering seams needs a new review. G'MIC's Seamcarve filters stay off the allowlist (G'MIC entry below) until a re-check against the record after 2027-03-06.
- **Red Eye stays user-picked with fixed color and geometry rules** (checked 2026-10-24, [record](patent-research.md)): a click or drag box picks the eye; fixed redness rules and growth from it pick the pixels; the fix desaturates and darkens. Eye or face detection, whole-image scans, learned models, eye-shape, iris or glint classification, eye-finding circle fits and a fix-all command need a new check.

## G'MIC engine

- **The vendored interpreter is built without its patch-based algorithms.** The patch correspondence search (`_matchpatch` in `CImg.h`: PatchMatch propagation plus random search, the two phases of Adobe US 8285055, to 2031-04-09) and patch-based inpainting (`inpaint_patch` in `gmic.cpp`) are compiled out with `patchy_gmic_no_patch_algorithms`, not merely hidden. Never build the engine without that definition; `gmic_engine_leaves_out_programs_and_patch_algorithms` pins it. A filter that would need them at some setting fails there with "Not available in this build". Non-patch `inpaint` (neighbour averaging, no patch search) and shared diffusion/morphology helpers stay (offered filters use them internally). The excluded `fx_inpaint*` filter wrappers remain blocked.
- **Filters are offered only from `patchy_filters_allowlist.txt`.** Left out by name (`NAMED` in `scripts/dev/gmic/build_allowlist.py`): every Inpaint filter; patch-based texture synthesis, smoothing and style transfer; Seamcarve (content-aware resizing; [record](patent-research-content-aware-scale.md): US 7477800 to 2027-03-06, then a re-check); interactive segmentation and colorization, including the lineart colorizers; neural-network filters; Blend [Seamless] (gradient-domain compositing); Blur [Motion] (a blur kernel shaped by a user-drawn point path with weight decay; Adobe US 9723204 claims 1 and 12, to 2035); Blur [Depth-of-Field] (a per-pixel blur level around a focus point; Adobe US 9019310 claim 1, to 2033); and Custom Code. Every other filter that runs in this build is offered. The named list is by rule; the family triage is in the record; adding a filter in those areas needs the same review as a native feature.
- **Point handles are plain position markers** (`CanvasWidget::set_preview_point_handles`): one per point the filter declares, never added or removed by Patchy, no per-marker amount or dial, nothing drawn around them (no extent, ring, boundary or region), and each only sets a number the dialog also shows. G'MIC's own preview commands (which draw guides, ellipses and the motion spline) are never run. Blur [Radial] and Blur [Angular] show their centre this way: a marker alone meets no independent claim of the Lens/Iris family above. `report_points()` gives Blur [Motion] and Blur [Depth-of-Field] no marker even if they were ever offered.
- **Enforce exclusions inside the interpreter.** `gmic_command_policy.hpp` replaces restricted apply, preview, private-helper and algorithm definitions with refusal stubs at registration; built-in patch `denoise` is refused at dispatch. This covers nested formula `run()`, aliases, copied bodies and redefinitions. Keep the core refusal tests and UI unchanged-layer test in [gmic.md](gmic.md). It does not cover user-written algorithms.
- **No external programs, no network, no other G'MIC code.** The engine is built with `gmic_noexec` and `cimg_no_system_calls` and without curl. Community filters and online updates are never loaded (mixed licensing, mutable after review).
- **Licensing.** Only the CeCILL-C sources are used. Nothing comes from G'MIC-Qt (GPL 3), and FFTW (GPL) is never linked; the engine's FFT is pocketfft (BSD-3-Clause).
